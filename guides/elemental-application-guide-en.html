Genshin Elemental Application Guide: GU, ICD & Aura Consumption Explained

Elemental application is the math actually running under every fight in Genshin. Why does the same team Vaporize every rotation for one player and whiff for another? The answer is not your stats — it is Gauge Units (GU) and Internal Cooldown (ICD). Here is both systems, end to end.

  • What is a Gauge Unit? Every elemental application carries a strength value in GU: 1U, 2U or 4U. 1 GU equals 25 units of Element Durability.
  • Why the 20% cut? An aura is taxed 20% the moment it lands: 1U becomes 0.8U, 2U becomes 1.6U, 4U becomes 3.2U.
  • How long does an aura last? Duration is (2.5 × GU + 7) seconds: 0.8U = 9.5s, 1.6U = 12s, 3.2U = 17s.
  • How does ICD work? Standard rule: after an application, no reapplication until 2.5 seconds pass or 3 more hits land — whichever comes first.
  • Do plunge attacks have ICD? No. Plunging attacks have zero ICD, so every single plunge applies its element.

Gauge Units and the 20% Aura Tax

Every elemental hit carries an invisible number: its Gauge Unit value. Most normal attacks and elemental skills apply 1U; elemental bursts and some charged attacks often apply 2U, with a few standalone applications at 4U.

ApplicationAura CreatedAura Duration
1U0.8U9.5 seconds
2U1.6U12 seconds
4U3.2U17 seconds

The formula is (2.5 × aura GU + 7) seconds. Note the 20% tax is deducted the instant the aura is created — so a 1U hit really only leaves a 0.8U aura behind.

Decay Rate Inheritance: Why Stacking Lasts Longer

When you reapply the same element, decay rate inheritance kicks in: the new aura keeps the higher GU value of the two, but inherits the decay rate of the initial application.

  • Apply 1U, then 2U → you get a 1.6U aura decaying at the slower 1U rate, lasting roughly 19 seconds — longer than a clean 2U application (12s).
  • Pyro is the exception: since Version 3.0, Pyro no longer inherits decay from previous stacks, so a new Pyro application decays at its own rate. You cannot extend a Pyro aura by layering.

This is why players who understand the system open with a 1U application to build a longer-lasting aura, while a single 2U hit can actually end up shorter.

ICD: The 2.5-Second Timer and 3-Hit Rule

ICD (Internal Cooldown) limits how often you can apply an element — not how often you deal damage.

  • Standard rule: the element applies on hit 1, then cannot apply again until 2.5 seconds pass or the 4th hit lands, whichever comes first.
  • Hard vs soft reset: when the 2.5s timer expires it clears both the timer and the hit counter (hard reset). When the 4th hit triggers it, only the hit counter clears — the timer keeps ticking (soft reset).
  • Worked example: Yoimiya's normal attacks apply Pyro on hits 1, 4 and 7 within one 2.5s window, i.e. the sequence (1, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0...).
  • Weapon type sharing: Claymore and Sword users share ICD between normal and charged attacks; Polearm, Bow and Catalyst users keep separate ICDs per attack type — which is how Hu Tao (polearm) alternates normals and charged attacks to maximise Pyro application.
  • Plunge attacks have zero ICD: every plunge applies its element, a big deal for plunge-heavy carries.

Aura vs Trigger: How Reactions Eat Gauges

Every reaction needs an element already on the enemy (the aura) and a second element landing on it (the trigger). Fixed coefficients decide how much of the aura is consumed.

ReactionGauge Consumption
Forward amplifying (Forward Vaporize / Forward Melt)2.0× the trigger's GU
Reverse amplifying (Reverse Vaporize / Reverse Melt)0.5× the trigger's GU
Transformative (Overloaded, Superconduct, Hyperbloom, etc.)1.0×
Swirl / Crystallize0.5×

This is why reverse Vaporize can sustain multiple Pyro triggers on a thin Hydro aura — each trigger only eats 0.5× — while forward reactions demand a thicker aura. Whoever applies first owns the aura, and that order decides who gets the multiplier.

Coexisting Auras and Special Cases

  • Quicken (Dendro + Electro): the two form a persistent Quicken aura that coexists independently. Later Electro hits trigger Aggravate, Dendro hits trigger Spread, and the aura is not consumed the usual way — the core shift in post-Sumeru combat.
  • Electro-Charged (Hydro + Electro): a per-second tick reaction consuming roughly 0.4U per second and draining both the Hydro and Electro auras at once.
  • Swirl: has a ~0.5 second ICD on its damage, so rapid Kazuha burst ticks do not all deal damage.
  • Burning (Pyro + Dendro): reapplies 1U Pyro every 2 seconds, creating a self-sustaining Pyro aura that can interfere with other setups.
  • Transformative reactions ignore crit and DMG bonus: Overloaded, Superconduct, Hyperbloom, Swirl and friends scale only with the trigger's level and Elemental Mastery, ignore defence, but are still affected by the matching elemental resistance.

Practical Takeaways

  • Decide aura and trigger first: settle who owns the aura and who eats the multiplier before arranging skills. It beats stat stacking.
  • Set auras with no-ICD characters: Xiangling, Xingqiu and Kokomi keep an aura up reliably.
  • Do not expect long-ICD abilities to apply on every hit: build around Yelan's and Oz's timers.
  • Plunge attacks are free application: when you need an element fast, plunging has no ICD.
  • Pyro auras cannot be extended: since 3.0, Pyro does not inherit decay, so Melt teams maintain the Pyro aura under the new rules.

Bottom Line

The whole system compresses into three rules. First, gauge units: 1U/2U/4U, taxed 20% on landing, lasting (2.5 × GU + 7) seconds. Second, ICD: 2.5 seconds or 3 hits, whichever comes first, with plunge attacks exempt. Third, whoever applies first owns the aura and the multiplier. Apply those three to team building and rotations and your reaction consistency improves immediately.
