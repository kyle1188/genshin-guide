Genshin Impact Cooking Guide: Proficiency, Special Dishes & Auto Cook

Cooking is the most underrated efficiency system in Teyvat — annoying day to day, then desperately needed before the Abyss. This guide covers the timing minigame, proficiency caps, special dish odds and cooking bonus characters, plus practical tips for processing and batch cooking.

  • What counts as Delicious? Stop the indicator in the orange zone for a Delicious dish (max effect, gains proficiency), yellow for Regular, gray for Suspicious. Exit before committing and no ingredients are consumed.
  • How much proficiency is needed? By rarity: 1★=5, 2★=10, 3★=15, 4★=20, 5★=25. Max it out to unlock Auto Cook.
  • What does Auto Cook do? Cooks up to 99 at once, always Delicious, and cannot be cancelled once started.
  • How do special dishes trigger? Only when the matching character cooks the dish, and only by chance — lower rarity dishes have higher odds (4★ still rolls 5%-10%).
  • Who doubles the output? Xiangling (ATK), Xinyan (DEF), Diona and Jean (recovery), Yun Jin (adventure) each have a 12% chance to double a perfect dish.

Basics: The Timing Minigame and Three Qualities

Cooking is a crafting method used to make Food and process ingredients. The first time you learn a dish you must perfectly cook it manually several times before Auto Cook unlocks.

Manual cooking is a timing minigame: stop the sweeping indicator inside the correct band.

  • Orange zone → Delicious: strongest effect, gains proficiency normally.
  • Yellow zone → Regular: average effect.
  • Gray zone → Suspicious: weakest effect.
  • Exiting early: ingredients are not consumed, so you can practice freely.

One trivia note: Raiden Shogun cannot cook — selecting her gives the message "This character cannot cook."

Every dish tracks its own proficiency. Cook it perfectly enough times and Auto Cook unlocks, retiring the minigame for good.

Auto Cook produces up to 99 servings, always Delicious and cannot be cancelled. Manual cooking stays available even at max proficiency, which matters if you ever want a specific quality on purpose.

Special Dishes: Odds and Triggers

A Special Dish is a character-exclusive variant with a stronger effect plus its own icon and name. It only triggers when the matching character cooks the matching dish, and it is always a chance roll — lower rarity dishes are far more generous.

Takeaway: always roll on Delicious quality — you get the best odds and still gain proficiency. To farm a specific special dish, batch-cook it with its character.

Where Ingredients Come From

  • Gathering: mushrooms, Sweet Flowers, carrots, berries and more are picked straight off the map; killing boars and fowl yields meat.
  • Shops: general stores and restaurants in each main city restock basic ingredients and seasonings daily — clear the shelves when you pass by.
  • Processing: Flour, Cheese, Cream, Sugar, Ham and similar items come only from the Processing tab and take real time — seconds for a few, close to an hour in bulk, so queue them early.
  • Expeditions: send characters on ingredient-yielding expeditions for a steady passive supply of meat and grains.

Where to Cook

  • City stoves: every regional capital has an outdoor stove, usually right next to the food vendor.
  • Campfires: light them with Pyro first; Anemo, Cryo, Electro, Geo and Hydro will extinguish them, and rain puts out open-air campfires — do not walk out in a storm expecting to cook.
  • Portable stove: a forged consumable gadget that lets you cook anywhere in the wild.
  • Serenitea Pot: multiple cooking furnishings can be placed in your realm (4 exterior, 5 interior) for home batch cooking.

Practical Batch Cooking Tips

  • Prep before the Abyss: cook a full round of ATK, DEF and recovery food up front instead of hunting for a stove between chambers.
  • Battle Pass wants 20 dishes weekly: cook the 20 cheapest dishes you know, not high-end food.
  • Co-op means eat your own: in single player food buffs cover the whole active party; in co-op a buff only applies to the eater, so every player must eat their own.
  • Max proficiency first, then stockpile: batch the same dish to hit max proficiency fast, then Auto Cook turns prep into a few clicks.
  • Stock by purpose: recovery for emergencies, buffs for the Abyss, stamina food for exploration — a full pantry you cannot use is just clutter.

Bottom Line

Cooking comes down to two habits: use the right character and batch for proficiency. Push your staple dishes to max proficiency to unlock Auto Cook, then prep becomes a few clicks. Swap in Xiangling, Xinyan or Diona for their 12% double-output talents, and do not forget the Battle Pass wants 20 dishes cooked every week.
