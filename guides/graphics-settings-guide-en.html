Genshin Impact Best Graphics Settings: PC and Mobile FPS Guide

Genshin Impact has more than a dozen graphics options, but only four genuinely decide your frame rate: render resolution, shadow quality, volumetric fog and reflections. The rest are mostly good visuals for a modest cost. This guide orders every option by how much it costs, gives three presets you can copy directly, and ends with a troubleshooting order for stutter.

Quick answer

  • Pick a stable target first: a locked 60 FPS beats a fluctuating 90. Frame pacing matters more than peak numbers.
  • Four biggest costs: render resolution, then shadows, volumetric fog and reflections. Lower these first when frames drop.
  • Best value options: anti-aliasing and bloom - visible improvement for a small cost.
  • Mid-range preset in one line: fullscreen, render resolution 1.0, shadows medium, volumetric fog off, reflections low, anti-aliasing on, motion blur off.
  • Mobile priority: cut render resolution and shadows first, turn off fog and reflections, then consider dropping 60 FPS to 45 or 30 for stability and lower temperatures.

The rule: lock the frame rate target first, then add visual quality back. If frames jump between 35 and 60, capping at 45 or 30 will feel considerably better than chasing 60.

Display mode, frame rate and V-Sync

  • Fullscreen performs best. Borderless windowed suits multi-monitor setups but usually costs a few frames.
  • FPS cap: the game offers 30 and 60, with higher options on PC where the hardware supports it. Never cap above what your GPU can sustain.
  • V-Sync: turn it off if your display supports variable refresh rate (G-Sync or FreeSync); otherwise keep it on to avoid tearing.
  • With variable refresh, cap slightly below your refresh rate for smoother frame pacing.

System and driver level tuning

  • GPU control panel (create a per-application profile, do not change global settings): set power management to prefer maximum performance, enable low latency mode, leave V-Sync to the in-game option, and do not force anti-aliasing or anisotropic filtering overrides.
  • Laptop dual GPU: assign the game to the dedicated GPU in Windows graphics settings, otherwise it may be running on integrated graphics.
  • Run plugged in: on battery, laptops cut GPU power heavily and the same settings can lose half their frame rate.
  • Install on an SSD: it mainly helps loading and texture streaming rather than frame rate.
  • Close background apps and capture software: browser tabs, recorders and stream encoders all compete for CPU and VRAM.
  • Update GPU drivers: most crashes, black screens and texture artifacts are solved by a driver update.

Mobile settings and thermals

SettingRecommendation
FPSIf the phone heats up or stutters, drop from 60 to 45 or 30 - it noticeably stabilizes the experience
Render ResolutionLower one step, the single most effective load reduction on mobile
Shadow Quality / Environment DetailLower each by one step
Volumetric Fog / ReflectionsTurn off wherever the toggles exist
Visual EffectsKeep at medium if possible; too low makes combat effects unreadable

Test properly: do not judge frame rate while standing in a quiet area. Play 15 to 20 minutes including combat, because mobile throttling kicks in after heat builds. Removing the phone case, avoiding direct sunlight and not playing while charging often help stability more than any settings change.

Bottom line

Use render resolution, shadows, volumetric fog and reflections to lock your frame rate target, then buy visual quality back with anti-aliasing and bloom. Beyond settings, the dedicated GPU, a wall outlet and decent cooling set the floor for laptop performance.

FAQ

How many FPS do you need in Genshin Impact?

30 is workable for exploration, 60 is recommended for combat and the Spiral Abyss. Frame pacing consistency matters more than the peak number.

Render resolution versus display resolution?

Both cut GPU load, but render resolution is an internal multiplier, so UI and text stay at native sharpness. That makes it look cleaner than simply dropping display resolution.

Should motion blur be on?

Off is the better default. It is a taste setting; turning it off sharpens camera movement and helps lower-end hardware a little.

Why do I stutter in cities but not in the wild?

Cities render many NPCs and lights at once, which loads CPU and VRAM. Lowering crowd density or environment detail helps more than touching GPU-heavy options.

How long can I play on a phone before taking a break?

Take a cooling break after roughly 40 to 60 minutes of continuous play. Sustained heat costs frames and accelerates battery aging.

When does cloud gaming make sense?

When your hardware is below minimum specs - an old laptop or an older phone. Cloud streaming runs max settings, at the cost of depending on network quality and latency.
