Genshin Overloaded Reaction Guide: 2.75x Scaling, 5m AoE and Knockback

Overloaded triggers when Pyro meets Electro. It has the second-highest multiplier of any transformative reaction (2.75), yet it is still called "big numbers, slow clears". This guide covers the scaling, the EM conversion, the 90 poise damage, the knockback problem, and why the 5.2 rebalance made Overload teams actually viable.

Quick Answer
  • Pyro on Electro or Electro on Pyro both trigger it, dealing Pyro DMG in a 5 m radius.
  • Damage = 2.75 x Level Multiplier. At level 90 the base is 3,979. It scales only with level, EM and the target's Pyro RES — it ignores DEF, cannot crit, and ignores ATK.
  • It also deals 90 poise damage plus heavy knockback, and counts as a Blunt Attack — so it can Shatter and breaks certain shields well.
  • The explosion does not apply Pyro, so it cannot chain by itself; you must re-apply an aura each time.
  • Version 5.2 raised the multiplier from 2.0 to 2.75, turning Overload from a meme into a real archetype.

Trigger Rules: Order Does Not Matter

  • Hit a Pyro-affected target with Electro, or
  • hit an Electro-affected target with Pyro.

Both directions deal identical damage, so build the rotation around whichever side is easier to apply. The reaction consumes both auras, which is why Overload teams need high-frequency application rather than two big hits.

Damage Formula and Level Table

Overloaded DMG = 2.75 x Level Multiplier x (1 + EM bonus) x target Pyro RES multiplier. The Level Multiplier follows the triggering character's level, so a level 90 trigger versus a level 70 trigger can more than double the output.

Note the third column: the same explosion hits for 3,308 when an enemy triggers it, versus 3,979 for the player. That gap is level suppression — and it also applies to you if your trigger is underleveled. It is the single most common reason a player sees "only a few thousand" per Overload.

Overload also ignores DEF, cannot crit, and ignores ATK and Pyro DMG Bonus. Only the target's Pyro RES modifies it. Crit stats do nothing for the reaction itself; they only serve the character's direct damage.

Do not forget RES: most enemies sit at 10% Pyro RES, so a level 90 zero-EM Overload realistically lands around 3,581. Against higher-RES targets it drops further, which is exactly why RES shred is so valuable here.

90 Poise Damage and Heavy Knockback

Overload is not just damage — it is a Blunt hit with 90 poise damage and strong knockback. For comparison:

The knockback cost is concrete: enemies get pushed out of your AoE, grouping is wasted, melee carries have to chase, and skills whiff. So "big numbers, slow clears" is not a myth — knockback converts damage into repositioning time.

Four ways to mitigate it:

  • Fight against walls — terrain eats the knockback distance.
  • Bring grouping — Kazuha, Venti or Sucrose pull enemies back after the explosion.
  • Play ranged or wide AoE — catalysts, bows and large burst zones do not care about scatter.
  • Run Chevreuse — her RES shred and healing make the scatter tolerable, which is the current standard fix.

What Overload Is Actually Good At

1. Interrupting

90 poise damage is mid-to-high among reactions, and high trigger frequency equals sustained suppression. Against Abyss Mage chants and Fatui charge-ups, Overload reliably cancels the animation — often worth more than the damage itself.

2. Breaking shields

Because it is a Blunt Attack, Overload handles Geo shields and most physical shields well; being Pyro damage, it also has an edge against Cryo shields. Against shielded elites, Overload teams often advance faster than raw damage teams.

3. Shatter and chain reactions

As a Blunt hit it can Shatter frozen targets, and it detonates explosive barrels in the world — both genuinely useful while exploring and clearing camps.

Team and Build

  • Core support: Chevreuse. Your party must contain only Pyro and Electro characters; after an Overload she shreds the team's targets' Pyro and Electro RES and heals. She is the entry ticket — without her the team still functions, but pays full price for the scatter.
  • On-field: Raiden Shogun, Arlecchino, Lyney, Clorinde — characters that keep an aura up while attacking.
  • Off-field: Xiangling, Beidou, Yae Miko, Fischl for application and sub-DPS.
  • Artifacts: 4-piece Thundering Fury or 4-piece Crimson Witch of Flames. Both give +40% Overloaded DMG; choose by the carry's direct damage type.
  • Stats: pure reaction teams prefer EM sands and goblet; hybrid teams keep an ATK sands and pick up EM from substats.

One rule decides everything: Overload damage uses the EM of the character who triggered it. Let your highest-EM unit trigger instead of letting the carry do it incidentally. Get this backwards and you lose roughly half your output.

FAQ

Why does Overload only happen once?

Because the explosion does not re-apply Pyro, the target is left with no usable aura. High-frequency appliers (Xiangling, Beidou, Fischl) therefore fit Overload far better than slow, high-multiplier units.

Can one target take repeated Overloads?

With limits: the same attacker can only deal Overload DMG to the same target once every 0.5 seconds, and multiple Overloads on one target within 0.1 seconds resolve only once regardless of source. Stacking trigger frequency has diminishing returns.

Does Overload benefit from Pyro DMG Bonus?

No. Elemental DMG Bonus only affects the character's own skills. Transformative reactions scale with level, EM and RES shred only — so upgrade the trigger, stack EM, or shred Pyro RES.

Can Overload Shatter a frozen enemy?

Yes. It is classified as a Blunt Attack, so it Shatters frozen targets and damages Geo shields efficiently.

Is Overload playable without Chevreuse?

Yes, but noticeably worse: you lose the 40% RES shred and nobody compensates for the scatter. Bring a reliable shielder or strong grouping first.

Bottom Line

Overload trades control for damage: a solid 2.75 multiplier on one side, 90 poise damage and knockback on the other. It is worth running when your enemies can be interrupted and your team can survive the scatter. If both answers are yes, it is the most cost-efficient AoE option in the game.
