Genshin Poise Guide: Poise Damage, Interruption Resistance and Break Windows

Poise is the most invisible system in Genshin and one of the most important for how a character feels. Why does the same unit sometimes get launched by one hit and other times eat a full combo? Why does a shield stop all interruption? This guide covers the poise bar, poise damage, hyperarmor multipliers and break windows.

Quick Answer
  • Every character and enemy has a hidden poise bar. Hits drain it by poise damage x incoming poise multiplier.
  • That multiplier = base endurance x hyperarmor, and sources multiply instead of adding: 0.3 x 0.5 = 0.15, so you only take 15%.
  • At zero the unit is poise broken; any sufficiently forceful hit then staggers or launches it.
  • Any shield sets the multiplier to 0 — regardless of remaining shield HP. Even a sliver of a Crystallize shield blocks all interruption.
  • Reaction poise damage: Electro-Charged 130 > Swirl primary 130 > Overloaded 90 > Superconduct / Burning / Shatter 30.
  • Poise is not elemental shields. Shields are broken with elemental gauge; poise is broken with poise damage.

None of these are published by HoYoverse; all values come from community testing. Once you know them, most "weird feel" complaints explain themselves.

The Multiplier Stacks Multiplicatively

Formula: actual poise damage = attack poise damage x base multiplier x hyperarmor₁ x hyperarmor₂ x ...

Example: Xingqiu's Raincutter has a 0.3 extra multiplier, Hu Tao's Paramita Papilio state has 0.5. Stacked, this is not 0.3 + 0.5 but:

0.3 x 0.5 = 0.15 — you only take 15% of incoming poise damage.

That is why stacking interruption resistance feels absurdly strong, and why adding a second layer to a unit that already has hyperarmor is worth far more than putting one layer on a bare unit. Conversely, if any term is 0 (shield, burst animation), the product is 0 and extra sources do nothing.

Two practical takeaways:

  • Bursts are free i-frames plus hyperarmor. Tanking a hit with a burst is often safer than dodging — and costs no stamina.
  • Plunging attacks are immune for the whole animation. "Plunge into charged attack" loops are therefore the safest option when surrounded.

Shields Grant Full Interruption Immunity

While any shield is up, the incoming multiplier is 0 — independent of remaining shield HP or shield strength. A thin Crystallize shield and Zhongli's Jade Shield are identical for interruption purposes; they differ only in absorption and duration.

So a shielder's value splits into three parts:

  • Interruption immunity (multiplier 0): active as long as the shield exists, no investment needed.
  • Damage absorption: scales with shield HP, level, HP% and Shield Strength.
  • Extra utility: Zhongli's RES shred, Diona's healing, Thoma's Pyro application, Layla's Cryo.

Conversely, protection vanishes the instant the shield breaks, with no grace period. Refresh early rather than waiting for it to crack.

Breaking Poise on Purpose

  • Claymores and charged attacks: highest single-hit poise damage, ideal for opening a fight.
  • Plunging attacks: high poise damage and immune throughout, the safest option when surrounded.
  • High-poise skills: some skills and bursts carry very high poise damage; exact values vary, so test in game.
  • Electro-Charged / Overloaded: the reactions themselves drain poise, so Taser and Overload teams break poise incidentally.
  • Launch skills (Jean's skill, Kazuha's skill): break poise first, then launch for bigger fall damage.

The reward for breaking poise is not damage — it is a window with no counterattack. The enemy staggers, your long rotation completes, and your casts do not get interrupted. A lesser-known detail: lower poise means higher launch velocity and heavier fall damage (up to 160%). That is why "drain poise, then launch" is the standard Jean and Kazuha technique.

Three Common Misconceptions

1. Poise is the same as elemental shields

No. Elemental shields (Abyss Mage Cryo shield, Fatui Electro shield, Geo Lawachurl armor) are drained by elemental gauge, so the right element breaks them fast. The poise bar is drained by poise damage and ignores element type entirely. Breaking a Cryo shield with Pyro has nothing to do with poise.

2. Breaking poise increases damage

It does not. Poise break only provides control (stagger, launch, interrupt) and adds no damage multiplier. Its value is letting you finish a rotation safely.

3. A shield means you can never be interrupted

Only while the shield exists. The moment it breaks, protection is gone — so refresh timing matters more than shield size, especially during long boss combos.

FAQ

Why does my character get launched when someone else's does not?

Different hyperarmor state: they may be inside a burst, a plunge, or a shield (multiplier 0) while you are bare. Level difference also affects the level coefficient applied to poise damage.

Does interruption resistance stack?

Yes, but multiplicatively. Two 0.5 sources give 0.25, not 0. A third layer adds little — bring a shield instead.

How long does a poise break last?

It depends on the enemy. Once broken, the bar stops draining, then refills and the stagger ends a few seconds later. That window is your burst phase — put your ultimate there.

Why do small enemies feel like they have no poise bar?

Their bars are short enough that one or two normal attacks break them, so they seem to fly on contact. Elites and bosses have far longer bars and require deliberate poise pressure.

Bottom Line

Poise comes down to one sentence: poise damage decides how fast you break them, the incoming multiplier decides how fast they break you, and a shield sets that multiplier to zero. Once you know those three, "do I need a shielder" and "should this carry build interruption resistance" stop being guesswork.
