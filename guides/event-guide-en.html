Genshin Event Guide: Patch Rhythm, Reward Structure & How to Never Miss One

Limited-time events are the most concentrated Primogem source in Genshin Impact — and the only one that expires for good. A single flagship event is worth roughly a month of daily commissions. This guide covers event types, reward structure, the rhythm of a patch, and the mistakes that cost players hundreds of Primogems.

  • How many events per patch? A patch runs about 42 days (6 weeks) and usually packs 1 flagship event plus 3–5 smaller ones, released in phases across the first and second half.
  • How much does a flagship event give? Roughly 1,000–2,000 Primogems in total, plus a free 4-star event weapon (fully refinable) and a Crown of Insight.
  • What about the smaller events? Combat and exploration events give about 420–960 Primogems; minigames give fewer but take almost no time.
  • What if I miss one? Event rewards cannot be claimed after they expire. Missing one patch's flagship event costs you about a month of daily commission Primogems.

A concrete example: the 7.0 Snezhnaya patch's flagship, Great Expeditionist Challenge, handed out a free Diona alongside a Crown of Insight, Hero's Wit and ascension materials, while the same patch also ran an exploration-collection event, a tower defence mode and a week of double domain drops.

The 42-Day Patch Rhythm

  • First half: one flagship event plus two smaller ones; the flagship typically spans the whole half and unlocks in stages.
  • Second half: one or two smaller events, usually combat or double-drop types.
  • Events do not all open at once: each has its own start and end date, so do not expect to clear everything on launch day.
  • Double-drop events last only a week: Ley Line Overflow and Overflowing Abundance windows are short, so plan your Original Resin around them.

How to Play a Patch Without Slipping

  • Neither rush nor drag: the flagship unlocks in stages, so clearing each day's stage as it opens is enough — the window is generous.
  • Finish 3 days early: leaving it to the final day is risky. Server hiccups or real-life interruptions can cost you the rewards entirely.
  • Three things on patch day: claim maintenance compensation (300–600 Primogems), redeem the three Special Program codes (about 300 Primogems), and start the highest-priority limited event.
  • Never buy Resin with Primogems: spending Primogems to buy Resin so you can grind event rewards is a net loss.

Special Program Codes

  • About 10–14 days before each patch, Hoyoverse runs the Special Program livestream revealing new characters, events and maps.
  • It releases three redemption codes, worth roughly 300 Primogems in total.
  • The codes usually expire within 24 hours, so redeem them immediately rather than waiting for patch day.
  • The stream is also the best time to plan your next patch's pulls and Resin, so it is worth watching live.

Reward Priority

  • Tier one: Primogems and Intertwined Fates — they feed your pulls, so they always come first.
  • Tier two: the Crown of Insight and the limited 4-star event weapon, both hard to replace later (event weapons generally do not return).
  • Tier three: Mora, EXP books and enhancement ore — ordinary resources that are never worth buying Resin for.

Mail and Claim Windows

  • Most event rewards arrive by mail, and mail is kept for 30 days before it disappears.
  • Some rewards must be claimed manually on the event page — clearing a stage is not the same as claiming it.
  • Web event rewards usually land in your mail within 24–48 hours of completion.

Common Mistakes

  • Last-day rushing: timeouts, unclaimed rewards and server lag can all waste the effort.
  • Forgetting the livestream codes: they expire fast, and three codes is 300 free Primogems.
  • Buying Resin to catch up: trading Primogems for Resin to farm event rewards is pure loss.
  • Never opening your mail: mail clears after 30 days and event rewards are not re-sent.
  • Assuming everything is live on day one: events open in phases on different dates, so checking early just wastes a trip.

Bottom Line

The rule for events is: finish 3 days early, and always take the scarce rewards first. Patches are the densest Primogem source in the game, and the only one you cannot go back to. Redeem the livestream codes and claim maintenance compensation on patch day, work through the stages as they unlock, and wrap up three days before the deadline — do that and every patch pays out in full.
