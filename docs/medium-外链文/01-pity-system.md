# Should You Ever Pay for a Genshin Impact Character? The Pity System, Calculated

*Most guides tell you what pity "is". Few tell you what it costs you, in hard numbers, when you're actually chasing one character all the way to its final constellation. This one does.*

---

I've spent the last few months digging into Genshin Impact's gacha math, and the uncomfortable truth is that the system is far more predictable than the FOMO would have you believe. Every pull is a 0.6% baseline for a five-star — and it stays 0.6% all the way until pull 74. Then it climbs, every pull, until pull 90, where it becomes a guaranteed five-star. No exception. Nothing can push you past 90.

That single fact changes the whole "should I spend money" question. You can always budget a hard ceiling instead of chasing an open-ended one.

## The three rules that actually matter

**1. Soft pity is a ramp, not a promise.** Starting at pull 74, the five-star rate jumps to roughly 6.5%, then about 12%, 18%, 24%, 30%, 36%... and by pull 89 it's near 96%. In practice, around 60–70% of five-stars land between pulls 74 and 85. Hitting the hard cap at 90 is less unlucky than it feels — about 15–20% of players do it in a given cycle.

**2. Pity carries, but only within the same pool.** Character event banners inherit their pull counter into the next character event banner. Weapon banners inherit into the next weapon banner. The standard banner inherits into itself. These three — plus the Chronicle Wish — are completely independent of each other. Your 60-pull head start in a character banner is worth nothing in a weapon banner. And critically: only actually pulling a five-star resets the counter. Letting a banner end, or switching between banners, never resets it.

**3. The 50/50 is where people bleed.** When you finally hit a five-star on a character event banner, it's a coin flip: 50% it's the featured character, 50% it's a standard-rarity five-star. If you lose, your *next* five-star — even on a future banner — is guaranteed to be the one you wanted. That guarantee travels with your account, not the banner. So "dumping" pulls into the wrong banner can burn your big-pity and throw you straight back into a coin flip.

Since version 5.0 there's also the small "caught light" mechanic: after a lost 50/50, there's a ~0.018% chance the result converts to the featured character. It's real, it's tiny, and it nudges your effective odds of a featured five-star to about **55%** rather than 50%.

## What a character actually costs

Here's the budget table I'd put in front of anyone deciding whether to spend.

| Goal | Pulls | Primogems |
|---|---|---|
| Soft-pity average (~76 pulls) | 76 | ~12,160 |
| One hard cap (90) | 90 | 14,400 |
| Worst case (lose 50/50, then full hard cap) | 180 | 28,800 |

Most players get the character somewhere around 12,000–13,600 primogems — roughly three months of free-to-play saving. The realistic worst case, if you lose the 50/50 *and* eat the full 90-pull hard cap, is 180 pulls, or 28,800 primogems.

In US dollars, at the best 2026 gift-pack bundle pricing that's about **$238 with the first-purchase 2x and ~$360 without it** for that 180-pull worst case. Plan your budget at 90 pulls (14,400) and you'll cover the situation for the vast majority of people.

## Constellations are where the spending gets real

Every constellation is a full second pity cycle — its own 50/50 plus its own hard cap. So going from C0 to C6 means pulling that character seven times.

- **C0 (the character itself):** ~12,160 primogems on average
- **C2:** ~36,480 on average (you need three five-stars)
- **C6 (max):** ~85,120 on average; worst case near $2,500 without the first-purchase bonus

My honest take: **C0 gives you about 80% of the experience for nearly every character.** Constellations are quality-of-life, not the difference between "playable" and "unplayable." Only a handful of characters have a true C1/C2 power spike worth chasing — and for those, the jump from C0 to C2 is the meaningful one, not C2 to C6.

## The one thing I'd never do

I'd never dump pulls into a weapon banner unless I was already committed to spending. Weapon pools are harsher than character pools: the hard cap is 80 instead of 90, it's a 75/25 (75% featured, 25% standard) instead of a 50/50, and the "lock-in" value resets to zero the moment the banner ends. The theoretical worst case in a weapon pool is 160 pulls for the featured weapon — and up to 240 if the first five-star you pull is the standard-weapon 25% case that earns no lock-in. Unless money is no object, character pools are the better value.
